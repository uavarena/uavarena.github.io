How a match works

Fifteen minutes. Four tasks. No mercy from the referee.

Points below are from the standard rules. Every rule lives in one configuration file, so leagues can change the format without touching code.

Take off

Each aircraft takes off inside the take-off window, flown by the team's own code or by a pilot from the 3D cockpit.

+10

Lock on

Hold an opponent inside your camera's hit zone for 4 seconds. Being locked on costs 2 points.

+25

QR dive

Dive at the ground pad and read its QR code from the camera image.

+5

Land

Land safely in the zone before time runs out. Geofence breaches, no-fly zones and unsafe flying cost points.

REFEREE EVIDENCE · T3 → T4 · VALID +10
Automatic referee

Every lock-on is checked against ground truth.

Each aircraft's forward camera is rendered as real images and streamed only to its own team. When a team claims a lock-on, the referee replays the camera projection, the hit zone and the hold time, and keeps a short evidence clip like this one.

  • Camera projection, hit zone, hold time and gaps verified
  • Live penalties for geofence, no-fly zones, unsafe proximity and false claims
  • Every telemetry message, claim and decision logged for replay

Under the hood

The same software that flies the real aircraft.

Real autopilots

ArduPilot SITL for every aircraft, PX4 alongside it, commanded over MAVLink.

Rendered cameras

Real images per aircraft, streamed as MJPEG to each team for its own detector.

Replays

Every match replayed in 3D with a timeline, events and 1–32× speed.

Fair play

Per-team tokens and an autopilot gateway that blocks forbidden commands and logs everything.

3D cockpit

Fly by hand from the browser with keyboard or gamepad, or with Mission Planner.

Built to scale

100 teams in one match with every team's full 5 Hz telemetry, measured.

Season modules

Beyond the competition format.

Optional modules for leagues that want electronic warfare in the air. Each one switches on or off per season.

Ground radar

Noise, clutter, radar horizon and a tracker; tracks offered to teams.

Hidden SAM sites

Track, lock, launch. Teams get a radar warning receiver to survive.

GPS jamming and spoofing

Zones where the position lies; detecting it is the team's job.

Comms jamming

Latency, loss and jitter per team, and jamming zones on the link.

Self-protection jammer

An ECM with a budget and cooldown, to spend wisely.

Fog of war

No free positions of opponents: find them with radar and camera.

Field notes

What test matches taught us.

Deconfliction before tactics.

Two aircraft running identical code collided 24 seconds after take-off.

Aim past the target.

A guided dive failed on every pass until the aim point moved beyond the QR pad; then every team read the code on its first dive.

Stagger the landings.

Two aircraft returning at the same moment met on final approach.
